02 · The trend

Rankings up, impressions down, clicks down

Sixteen months of organic clicks in the three markets that have sixteen months of data. Two things are visible at once: an enormous December, and a 2026 that sits consistently below 2025 despite average position improving by four to six places in every market.

How to read this. Position improving from ~13 to ~8.5 while impressions fall 20–40% is the signature of Google serving the site for fewer but better-matched queries — the long tail disappearing. That is consistent with AI Overviews absorbing informational queries, with a core update, or with the indexing situation in §05. It is not consistent with a tracking fault, because the shape is the same in three independent properties. Whatever the cause, the commercial fact stands: roughly a fifth of organic clicks have gone from the two largest markets, and Q4 is about to multiply whatever the run-rate is by three.

04 · AI search verdict

The AU pilot cannot answer the question

AI Search Optimisation went live on AU on 24 June, with HowTo schema on 26 June. The roadmap has a phase-1 rollout to the remaining four stores in Sprint 4, implicitly justified by the AU result. Here is the AU result, with the other four markets as a control group over identical dates.

Change in clicks per day · 24 Jun–1 Aug vs 1 May–23 Jun
AU is the only market where AI Search Optimisation was deployed in this window. The other four are the control.
Show the numbers
The verdict: no signal, in either direction. AU clicks per day fell 19.1%. The control group fell 11.3% on average — but the control range runs from CA at −21.0% to UK at +1.9%. AU's movement sits comfortably inside that spread. On impressions AU actually did better than the control (−17.0% against −20.9%). Two contradictory readings from the same deployment is what "not enough data" looks like.

Why AU could never have answered it

AU produced 745 clicks in 106 days — about 1% of estate organic. Detecting a 10% effect on a base of ~7 clicks a day needs months, not six weeks. The pilot was run in the market where the answer is least measurable.

There is a second problem: Search Console does not report AI Overview or AI Mode appearances as a separate dimension. Clicks from AI surfaces are folded into web search. So even with a bigger market, GSC clicks are the wrong instrument for measuring AI search visibility.

What to do instead

1. Do not let the AU number justify or block the rollout. It says nothing. Decide the Sprint 4 rollout on cost and strategic view, and write that reasoning into the Decision Log so it is honest.

2. Measure it somewhere with power. If a real read matters, the next phase belongs on UK or US, where a 10% move is visible in weeks.

3. Check the HowTo schema is doing anything. No HowTo appearance shows in any property's search-appearance data. Google retired HowTo rich results some time ago; if the justification is LLM parsing rather than rich results, that is a legitimate reason — but it should be written down, because it changes how success is defined.

One thing the AU data does show clearly. Rich-result impressions collapsed across the deployment window: merchant listings 59 → 10 impressions, review snippets 341 → 68. Small absolute numbers, but a schema change followed by a fall in rich-result eligibility is worth ten minutes in the Rich Results Test before the same code goes to four more stores.

Four in five known pages are not indexed

EU · 484 pages where Google overrode your canonical

Every other storefront has between 5 and 11. EU also runs language subfolders (sculpd.eu/de/…) and has nl. and de. subdomains in its crawl log. This is the hreflang and canonical problem in §12 of the roadmap, made measurable. US · 450 pages excluded by noindex

CA has 87. UK and EU have 1 each. A 450-to-1 difference between storefronts running nominally the same theme is not a content decision, it is drift — and it is exactly the kind of divergence the shared-theme programme exists to stop. Worth thirty minutes to confirm the exclusions are deliberate.

06 · Crawl health

The US store spends a sixth of its crawl budget on 404s

Googlebot made 153,154 requests to sculpd.com in the reporting window. 16.2% of them returned 404. Only 3.6% of crawling was discovery of new pages — the rest was refreshing what Google already knows about.

Googlebot response codes, share of all requests
A healthy estate sits above 90% on 200. Two of five storefronts do not.

Show the numbers
The AU property is inside the US property. The US crawl log lists au.sculpd.com with 17,891 requests — the exact total of the AU property. AU is a subdomain of sculpd.com, so US Search Console figures include AU traffic. Any baseline that reports US and AU as separate numbers is double-counting. This has to be resolved before the Sprint 1 baseline is signed off, or every subsequent report inherits the error. The cleanest fix is a URL-prefix property for sculpd.com excluding the AU subdomain, or reporting AU as a subset of US throughout.
Other hosts worth knowing about. Every storefront has satellite hosts being crawled — gifting. on four of five, plus help., account., book., careers. and retail.. All five primary hosts report "problems in the past". None of these are on the board, and the gifting. subdomains in particular deserve attention going into a gifting quarter.
07 · Opportunities

Where the clicks are, and are not

Merchant listings convert 5–15× better than product snippets — and get almost no impressions
Click-through rate by search appearance type. Bar height is CTR only — impression volumes are in the table below, and they are the point.
Product snippets Merchant listings
The clearest opportunity in this dataset. UK merchant listings run at 14.3% CTR from 4,516 impressions, against product snippets at 1.33% from 1.77 million. The rich result that converts ten times better is being shown for a quarter of one percent of the impressions. That is a Merchant Center feed and product-schema completeness problem, it is fixable, and it lands directly in the SEO and structured-data work already scheduled for Sprint 5. The US is the exception and the anomaly — 2.74% merchant listing CTR against 14–22% everywhere else, on 39,428 impressions. Something is materially different about the US feed. Worth an hour.
The non-brand head terms
Highest-impression non-brand queries, US and UK. Ranked by impressions, because that is where the unrealised clicks are.
"Candle making kit" is the single biggest unrealised term in the estate. 156,218 impressions in the US at position 7.8 and a 0.61% CTR; 103,311 in the UK at position 7.4. Moving that one query into the top three in both markets is worth more traffic than most of the SEO backlog combined — and it is a gifting-adjacent term that peaks precisely in the December window §03 identifies. The same pattern repeats on "pottery kit", "air dry clay kit" and "at home pottery kit".

08 · Data caveats

Read these before quoting any number above

01

AU and EU have no history before 18 April 2026

Both exports were requested as "Last 16 months" and both returned 106 days. The properties were almost certainly created in April 2026. No year-on-year comparison is possible for AU or EU this year, and neither has a Q4 2025 baseline. Their first real YoY read is April 2027.

Do Confirm the property creation dates, and check whether an older property for either domain exists under a different account. If it does, that history is recoverable.
02

US includes AU

Confirmed via the crawl log: au.sculpd.com sits inside the sculpd.com property. US totals are inflated by roughly the whole AU volume — small in absolute terms (745 clicks against 70,309) but it makes "US" and "AU" non-additive.

Do Decide the reporting convention now, before the baseline is signed off, and write it into the Metrics database definition.
03

Exports cap at 1,000 rows

UK and US queries and pages both hit exactly 1,000 rows, so the long tail is truncated. Totals in the query and page tables will not reconcile with the chart totals, which are complete.

Do Use the chart figures for anything reported. For deeper query analysis, the Search Console API or the Looker Studio connector removes the cap.
04

Two files were swapped in the export

UK-performance-16mo.zip contained the page-indexing report and UK-page-indexing.zip contained the performance data. Corrected in this analysis; worth renaming in the folder so the next person is not caught by it.

Do Also rename US-performance-16mo.zip.zip.
05

The GSC data-integrity ticket is not visible in this data

Ticket E3 says a deployment broke Search Console reporting. Nothing in these exports shows an obvious discontinuity — the daily series are continuous in all five properties. That does not mean the ticket is wrong; it means the problem is somewhere these exports do not reach, most likely a specific report, a sitemap, or a property-level setting.

Do Note precisely which report looked wrong and when. The fix in Week 0 needs a reproducible symptom, and right now the ticket does not have one.
