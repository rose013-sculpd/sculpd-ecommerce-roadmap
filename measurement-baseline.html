01 · Headlines

Sixteen findings, in the order they matter

Each one is either a number that should be in the roadmap and is not, or a number that contradicts something the roadmap currently assumes. The first five are all measurement faults — which is uncomfortable, and also the best possible news, because they are cheap to fix and everything downstream depends on them.

02 · Measurement

GA4 records that a sale happened. It does not record what it was worth.

Across all five properties, all twelve months and every single channel, GA4 reports total revenue of zero. Item revenue: zero. Items purchased: zero. Purchase counts and transaction counts are recorded correctly, and view_item and add_to_cart both work. The purchase event is reaching GA4 without its value, currency or items payload.

What this means in plain terms. Shopify still knows what Sculpd earns — the money is not missing and the business is not blind. What is missing is attribution: which channel, which campaign, which landing page, which product produced the revenue. And because AOV is a derived metric, it means two of the three commercial success metrics in your job specification — increased AOV and higher revenue per visitor — cannot be calculated from analytics today.
What is and is not being captured
12 months, 1 Aug 2025 – 31 Jul 2026, from the GA4 ecommerce purchases and traffic acquisition reports.

Why this is the top of the list

Every AOV mechanic in the H2 roadmap — multibuy in Sprint 3, cart and post-purchase upsells in Sprint 4, the gift builder in Sprint 5 — has AOV as its success metric. So does the bundles work. So does the CRO programme scheduled for Sprint 3, whose entire purpose is to run tests and prove the result.

None of them can be measured until this is fixed. That makes GA4 revenue tracking not the highest-leverage item in the plan but a blocker on roughly 60 hours of scheduled work. It should move ahead of everything except the Summer Sale price block.

How to diagnose it — about 30 minutes

1. Open GA4 DebugView, place a test order on one store, and watch the purchase event land. Check whether value, currency and items[] are present.

2. On Shopify the usual cause is the checkout / order-status pixel: a Customer Events web pixel that fires the event name but does not build the payload, or a GTM tag mapping that lost its variables. Check Settings → Customer events.

3. Confirm the currency code is valid on each property — an unrecognised currency silently zeroes revenue.

4. Fix one store, verify, then replicate. This is a five-store change, so it is also a shared-theme argument.

Two more measurement faults, both cheap to fix

A

Key events are misconfigured on CA, EU and US

3 of 5 stores

Session key event rate: CA 93.6%, EU 93.8%, US 94.6% — against AU 3.2% and UK 3.7%. A key event firing on nine sessions in ten is not a conversion, it is a page view that somebody marked as one. AU and UK are configured correctly, with the key event matching purchase count almost exactly. On the other three, every "conversion rate" GA4 displays is meaningless.

Fix Admin → Events → unmark whatever is flagged on CA, EU and US, and mark purchase only. Ten minutes per property. Until then, ignore every key-event metric on those three stores and use purchases ÷ sessions instead, as this report does.
B

GA4 data retention is almost certainly set to 2 months

Do this today

Every exploration returned roughly two months of data despite a twelve-month date range being requested, while the standard reports returned the full twelve. That is the signature of event-level data retention set to the 2-month default: standard reports read pre-aggregated tables, explorations read event-level data.

Fix Admin → Data settings → Data retention → 14 months, on all five properties. It takes sixty seconds and it is not retroactive — every day it stays at two months is another day of history permanently lost. This is the single most time-sensitive item in this document; do it before you finish reading.
03 · Traffic mix

Four in five sessions are paid

This reframes where optimisation effort is worth spending. Organic search — the channel the entire SEO and AI Search programme serves — is between 1.1% and 3.8% of sessions depending on the store. Paid, including cross-network, is between 75% and 85%.

The honest consequence for the roadmap. A 20% year-on-year fall in organic clicks is a fall in 3% of traffic. That is worth diagnosing — it is cheap and it may be an early signal — but it is not the Critical-tier commercial risk it looked like from Search Console alone, and I have downgraded it in the roadmap accordingly. 04 · Conversion

The UK converts two to three times better than everywhere else

Purchases divided by sessions, calculated directly rather than taken from the broken key-event metric. Same products, same nominal theme, five storefronts.

Session conversion rate by store and device
June–early August 2026, the only window explorations could return. Mobile is 66–74% of sessions everywhere.
Mobile Desktop
Show the numbers
The biggest number in this document. UK mobile converts at 4.76%. US mobile converts at 1.94% on 53,760 sessions in the same window. If the US store converted like the UK store, that two-month sample alone would have produced roughly 1,500 additional orders. Some of the gap is real — the UK is the home market, brand awareness is higher, and the traffic mix differs. But a 2.5× gap between two storefronts running nominally the same theme is not all brand, and it is a far more concrete CRO programme than a generic test backlog: the hypothesis is already written, and the control already exists and is live.
Device behaviour is inconsistent across stores, which is itself a finding. UK and EU convert better on mobile than desktop; CA is the reverse (desktop 2.88%, mobile 1.48%); US is flat. Five stores behaving five different ways on the same product range points at store-level differences rather than customer behaviour — exactly what the shared-theme programme is meant to eliminate.
05 · The funnel

Where each store loses people

From the GA4 funnel explorations, June to early August. Two storefronts, both device segments. The shape of the loss is different in each, which means the fix is different in each.

Show the numbers

US desktop checkout completes at 25%

Against 73% on UK desktop and 64% on US mobile. Three-quarters of US desktop users who begin checkout do not finish. On a store where desktop is a third of sessions, that is a large and specific leak.

Caveat worth checking first: the US funnel's third step is labelled "Checkout" and the UK's "Begin Checkout". If those are different events, the comparison does not hold. Confirm the step definitions match before acting — and then make all five funnels identical, because inconsistent funnel definitions are their own measurement problem.

US add-to-cart fell sharply in July

US desktop ATC rate went 14.24% → 8.62% between June and July; mobile 7.09% → 5.44%. The UK fell too but far less — mobile 12.69% → 10.81%. The US fell roughly twice as hard. US session conversion over the same months went 2.12% → 1.72%, while CA and AU both improved.

Worth an hour: the Summer Sale 2026 theme went live on 14 July. That may be coincidence — sale periods shift browsing mix — but a 40% drop in add-to-cart on the largest store, concentrated in the month a theme shipped, is worth ruling out before peak rather than after.

Add-to-cart is not the estate-wide problem. Across twelve months of item data the view-to-cart rate is broadly consistent — AU 9.2%, UK 8.6%, US 8.0%, CA 7.0%, EU 6.8%. The divergence appears after the cart, and it appears differently in each store. That argues for cart-and-checkout work over PDP work as the first CRO target, which is the opposite of where most test backlogs start.

08 · AI search verdict

The AU pilot cannot answer the question

AI Search Optimisation went live on AU on 24 June, with HowTo schema on 26 June. The roadmap has a phase-1 rollout to the remaining four stores in Sprint 4, implicitly justified by the AU result. Here is the AU result, with the other four markets as a control group over identical dates.

Change in clicks per day · 24 Jun–1 Aug vs 1 May–23 Jun
AU is the only market where AI Search Optimisation was deployed in this window. The other four are the control.
Show the numbers
The verdict: no signal, in either direction. AU clicks per day fell 19.1%. The control group fell 11.3% on average — but the control range runs from CA at −21.0% to UK at +1.9%. AU's movement sits comfortably inside that spread. On impressions AU actually did better than the control (−17.0% against −20.9%). Two contradictory readings from the same deployment is what "not enough data" looks like.

Why AU could never have answered it

AU produced 745 clicks in 106 days — about 1% of estate organic. Detecting a 10% effect on a base of ~7 clicks a day needs months, not six weeks. The pilot was run in the market where the answer is least measurable.

There is a second problem: Search Console does not report AI Overview or AI Mode appearances as a separate dimension. Clicks from AI surfaces are folded into web search. So even with a bigger market, GSC clicks are the wrong instrument for measuring AI search visibility.

What to do instead

1. Do not let the AU number justify or block the rollout. It says nothing. Decide the Sprint 4 rollout on cost and strategic view, and write that reasoning into the Decision Log so it is honest.

2. Measure it somewhere with power. If a real read matters, the next phase belongs on UK or US, where a 10% move is visible in weeks.

3. Check the HowTo schema is doing anything. No HowTo appearance shows in any property's search-appearance data. Google retired HowTo rich results some time ago; if the justification is LLM parsing rather than rich results, that is a legitimate reason — but it should be written down, because it changes how success is defined.

One thing the AU data does show clearly. Rich-result impressions collapsed across the deployment window: merchant listings 59 → 10 impressions, review snippets 341 → 68. Small absolute numbers, but a schema change followed by a fall in rich-result eligibility is worth ten minutes in the Rich Results Test before the same code goes to four more stores.

10 · Crawl health

The US store spends a sixth of its crawl budget on 404s

Googlebot made 153,154 requests to sculpd.com in the reporting window. 16.2% of them returned 404. Only 3.6% of crawling was discovery of new pages — the rest was refreshing what Google already knows about.

Googlebot response codes, share of all requests
A healthy estate sits above 90% on 200. Two of five storefronts do not.

Show the numbers
The AU property is inside the US property. The US crawl log lists au.sculpd.com with 17,891 requests — the exact total of the AU property. AU is a subdomain of sculpd.com, so US Search Console figures include AU traffic. Any baseline that reports US and AU as separate numbers is double-counting. This has to be resolved before the Sprint 1 baseline is signed off, or every subsequent report inherits the error. The cleanest fix is a URL-prefix property for sculpd.com excluding the AU subdomain, or reporting AU as a subset of US throughout.
Other hosts worth knowing about. Every storefront has satellite hosts being crawled — gifting. on four of five, plus help., account., book., careers. and retail.. All five primary hosts report "problems in the past". None of these are on the board, and the gifting. subdomains in particular deserve attention going into a gifting quarter.

12 · Data caveats

Read these before quoting any number above

01

AU and EU have no history before 18 April 2026

Both exports were requested as "Last 16 months" and both returned 106 days. The properties were almost certainly created in April 2026. No year-on-year comparison is possible for AU or EU this year, and neither has a Q4 2025 baseline. Their first real YoY read is April 2027.

Do Confirm the property creation dates, and check whether an older property for either domain exists under a different account. If it does, that history is recoverable.
02

US includes AU

Confirmed via the crawl log: au.sculpd.com sits inside the sculpd.com property. US totals are inflated by roughly the whole AU volume — small in absolute terms (745 clicks against 70,309) but it makes "US" and "AU" non-additive.

Do Decide the reporting convention now, before the baseline is signed off, and write it into the Metrics database definition.
03

Exports cap at 1,000 rows

UK and US queries and pages both hit exactly 1,000 rows, so the long tail is truncated. Totals in the query and page tables will not reconcile with the chart totals, which are complete.

Do Use the chart figures for anything reported. For deeper query analysis, the Search Console API or the Looker Studio connector removes the cap.
04

Two files were swapped in the export

UK-performance-16mo.zip contained the page-indexing report and UK-page-indexing.zip contained the performance data. Corrected in this analysis; worth renaming in the folder so the next person is not caught by it.

Do Also rename US-performance-16mo.zip.zip.
05

GA4 explorations returned ~2 months, not 12

Every exploration-based export (monthly KPIs, device splits, funnels) covers roughly June to early August 2026 despite a twelve-month range being requested. Standard reports returned the full year. Conversion and funnel figures in Section 04 and Section 05 are therefore a two-month sample, not an annual baseline, and contain no Q4.

Do Change data retention to 14 months, then re-run the explorations in September — you will have three months of comparable history and a real baseline by Sprint 2.
06

Exploration metric sets differ per property

AU and CA returned sessions/purchases/revenue; EU returned average daily purchasers; UK returned engaged sessions and engagement rate; US returned session key event rate. UK had no monthly-KPI export at all, so its figures here are derived from the device breakdown.

Do Build one exploration template and copy it to all five properties, so the next pull is genuinely comparable. This is a 20-minute job that pays for itself the first time you run the weekly report.
07

The GSC data-integrity ticket is not visible in this data

Ticket E3 says a deployment broke Search Console reporting. Nothing in these exports shows an obvious discontinuity — the daily series are continuous in all five properties. That does not mean the ticket is wrong; it means the problem is somewhere these exports do not reach, most likely a specific report, a sitemap, or a property-level setting.

Do Note precisely which report looked wrong and when. The fix in Week 0 needs a reproducible symptom, and right now the ticket does not have one.
